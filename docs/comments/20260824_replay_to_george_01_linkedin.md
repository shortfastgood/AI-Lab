When relying entirely on artificial intelligence, it is essential to consider how it must be trained in order to meet the needs of its users. In the case of code generation, this requires thousands of projects and millions of lines of code, often with no clear knowledge of their quality or relevance.
When AI is asked to generate code, the process can become little more than pulling the lever of a slot machine repeatedly until the task is complete. If the user does not have the skills needed to judge whether the result is any good, using that code remains a gamble.
Artificial intelligence does not invent anything, and it cannot solve a problem for which there are not yet enough data or examples. A non-engineer cannot overcome that limitation. Instead of contributing new and innovative solutions, they risk increasing the number of mediocre examples and pulling the whole system towards mediocrity.
Experience remains important. It cannot be acquired quickly, effortlessly, or simply through a tool.
