We tend to believe that artificial intelligence can understand and fix everything, but it is limited by the data it was trained on, however large the model may be. For a complex program and a specific error in it, the AI has to find a match in its data; if it cannot, the analysis it proposes will be wrong, and the fix even worse.

Frontier models help us far less than models specifically trained for a purpose. They also run locally, let us build a stable environment, and force us to understand what we are building.

I have been building software with both frontier and local models for nearly two years now. Both analyse and fix a great many of the very problems they created themselves, and they do it quickly. In mixed code, bugs and problems often go unspotted, and the code is sometimes changed in ways that create more problems than they solve.

Starting a production software project with AI is genuinely easy. Bringing it to maturity, efficiently and cost-effectively, is far more complicated and, for now, still beyond the reach of artificial intelligence.
