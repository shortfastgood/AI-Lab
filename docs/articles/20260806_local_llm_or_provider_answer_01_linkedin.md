My main audience is users who already buy that extra RAM, because they need it for other things; and those who cannot use remote models for clear contractual reasons. For other use cases, and the casual user, a remote model is still an excellent choice, as long as you accept the operating conditions.

My rule: why reach for a resource-heavy tool when a much smaller, faster model does the job locally?

I can recolor the body of a Chevrolet Bel Air in a 1791 x 703 image in 14 seconds, with a quantized Black Forest Labs model using just 13 GB of RAM. No remote model matches that, because of latency; and the result is even better, because the local model is specialized for image editing.

We run thousands of operations like this. On a MacBook Pro M4 Max with 64 GB, I found I could become partly independent of remote models, with real savings. Even when a bigger model arrives, I can keep the one I have today, since it already meets all my needs.

It's an oppotunity and not a solution for every one.
