# Experiences and Outcomes

One of the most important observations concerns the organisation and maintenance of project knowledge. Conventional methodologies are designed for human teams and remain well beyond the interpretative capacity of artificial intelligence (AI).

Their implementation is often dispersed and fragmented across multiple systems and formats. Large projects also face the problem of information becoming outdated or inconsistent over time.

This makes the effective integration of AI into a project difficult, if not impossible. What is needed instead is a methodology that actively supports such integration.

One idea gaining traction is the **[LLM Wiki](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)** proposed by Andrey Karpathy and later echoed by Google in its proposal for the **[Open Knowledge Format (OKF)](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/data-analytics/how-the-open-knowledge-format-can-improve-data-sharing?hl=en)**.

## Context

The context in which AI is expected to operate is fundamental if meaningful results are to be achieved. The starting point lies in the core instructions provided to the tool in use, such as `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md` and `copilot-instructions.md`. A useful reference is the example provided by [multica.ai](https://github.com/multica-ai/andrej-karpathy-skills), which builds on observations made by Andrey Karpathy.

Expanding the context with large amounts of additional information, such as skills, has so far failed to produce tangible improvements. The main difficulty is that the human operator cannot fully control either the positive or the negative interactions generated by skills, prompts and plans.

These additional instructions also generate a considerable number of tokens, which either increase costs in the case of large models or reduce the useful context window in the case of local ones.

A concrete plan or worked example is more effective than a large collection of skills because it can be understood immediately both by the AI and by the person directing it.

## Planning

Planning with the support of artificial intelligence, by developing ideas and decisions in successive stages, lays the foundations for a productive process in which AI can genuinely accelerate delivery. The use of such plans also enables AI to produce the expected result with fewer inconsistencies.

Where the tool in use includes a `plan` function or mode, this is generally the simplest option. Where it does not, AI can still be guided through a short written instruction.

It remains necessary, however, to retain control of the planning process and, in this respect, building an LLM Wiki, as suggested in the introduction, may prove highly valuable.

## Workflow

The step-by-step introduction of AI into the software delivery cycle, covering code writing, integration, testing, correction and retesting, has produced more problems than genuine advantages. Developers tend to check everything again, thereby cancelling out much of the benefit AI is intended to provide.

The cycle therefore needs to be automated, together with its controls and monitoring, in order to make proper use of AI's speed of execution.

### Steps

- Generation of code from a specification, a plan or an example.
- Generation of tests with coverage of at least 80%.
- Generation of test data designed to cover most user scenarios.
- Automated execution of tests and immediate AI-driven correction of errors.

### Control and Monitoring

- Verification of metrics through an established and reliable tool.
- Verification of test coverage through an established and reliable tool.
- Resource monitoring during a real test run involving a significant number of simulated users.

### Evaluation

From both a technical and an economic perspective, the implementation must satisfy all agreed functional and non-functional criteria. Greater emphasis should therefore be placed on verification and monitoring as the primary means of ensuring quality and resilience.

It is necessary to move away from manually checking every generated line of code and to recognise that a fully AI-based automated process cannot be established quickly or without the necessary experience.

### Reviews

Code can be reviewed at any stage with the aim of improving performance, but this process should remain separate from production. A rapid cycle of the kind enabled by intelligent automation makes it possible to test variants quickly and identify a better one.

AI has proved highly efficient at identifying critical or outdated patterns in code, regardless of the language involved. Even so, this activity still requires a developer with solid experience both in software and in the use of AI.
