## Introduction

This project began in 2023, as conversational AI entered the mainstream. Chat-based interfaces, led by ChatGPT, materially changed how artificial intelligence was used in practice. What began as an exploration of conversational systems evolved into a structured assessment of AI-augmented development workflows and their practical engineering value.

Originally, this repository collected experiences with GitHub Copilot and OpenAI's ChatGPT. Those services marked the first large-scale integration of AI into daily workflows, but they also exposed a structural dependence on centrally hosted systems whose benefits come with recurring financial and operational costs. As adoption widened, the question ceased to be whether these tools were useful and became whether their cost model was sustainable.

For individuals, that cost is often experienced as a steady accumulation of subscriptions, usage caps and limited control over the systems that mediate creative and technical work. For organisations, the same pattern expands into procurement complexity, compliance obligations, uncertain operating expenditure and growing dependence on external platforms for core internal processes. Once AI becomes part of routine work, the economics of access matter as much as model quality.

The social costs are less visible but no less consequential. Cloud-first adoption concentrates capability, data and decision-making power within a small number of providers, while normalising the transfer of working materials into infrastructures controlled elsewhere. In that setting, questions of privacy, autonomy and accountability become inseparable from questions of productivity.

It is within this context that locally run language models have become increasingly relevant. What was once a niche experiment reserved for specialist hardware is now a credible option for a growing set of practical tasks, including drafting, summarisation, code assistance and structured analysis. Local deployment does not remove trade-offs in latency, maintenance or peak capability, but it materially changes the balance between performance, governance and cost.

Evaluating local models is therefore no longer a purely technical exercise. It is a strategic response to rising usage costs, stricter data-handling requirements and the broader social implications of concentrating AI capability behind remote services. For many use cases, the decisive question is not whether a local model surpasses the largest hosted systems, but whether it delivers sufficient capability at a lower financial, organisational and societal cost.

This repository serves both as a historical record of that transition and as a practical guide for teams adopting AI-augmented workflows. It explores benefits, challenges and strategic considerations, while providing examples and pragmatic guidance for integrating AI agents into development processes.

The question is no longer whether AI will transform software development, but how rapidly organisations can adapt to exploit these capabilities while managing associated risks. This repository documents that ongoing transformation.
